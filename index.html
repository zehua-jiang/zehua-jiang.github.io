Zehua Jiang

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at New York University, advised by Prof. Julian Togelius at the NYU Game Innovation Lab. Before that I earned an M.S. in Electrical Engineering at New York University and a B.S. in Optoelectronic Information Science and Engineering at Sun Yat-sen University, including an exchange semester at UC Berkeley.

I study how agents build playable game levels from design goals by learning through game interaction, reusing pretrained knowledge, and choosing generation tools. My work spans reinforcement learning, open-ended generation from natural language, and reliable local language models, supporting both game creation and agent training. Earlier I worked on synthetic fingerprints and irises.

Outside research I am a traveler, photographer and video editor.

Research

From learned editors to tool-using agents

These projects explore reinforcement learning for controllable level generation and generalization across maps, open-ended generation from natural-language requests, and reliable execution with local language models.

  1. 01

    Learning Controllable 3D Level Generators

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Michael Cerny Green, Julian Togelius

    Extends PCGRL to Minecraft tasks with jumping and gravity. Editing policies learn structures such as staircases from task feedback and adapt them to different fixed-door configurations, without authored level examples. Control is stronger for shorter path targets and remains limited for jump counts and large vertical separations.

  2. 02

    Scaling, Control and Generalization in Reinforcement Learning Level Generators

    Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Julian Togelius

    We introduce a JAX implementation that parallelizes simulation and learning on the GPU, removing CPU–GPU transfer overhead to substantially accelerate training and enable experiments with one billion timesteps. We also introduce randomized map shapes and fixed key-tile positions for greater control, and show how local observations improve generalization to unseen maps.

  3. 03

    Video Game Level Design as a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Problem

    Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Eugene Vinitsky, Julian Togelius

    We formulate level generation as a cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning problem, with agents jointly editing a shared level. Multiple edits share each reward computation, reducing evaluation overhead. Experiments show improved generation efficiency and generalization to unseen map shapes.

  4. 04

    Agentic PCG: Procedural Content Generation via Tool-using LLMs

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Ahmed Khalifa, Julian Togelius

    A language model builds and revises levels through tools, using symbolic observations and feedback after each action. Individual tile edits and higher-level generation algorithms share one interface, with evaluators deciding which revisions to keep. The framework supports computable targets and broader language requests across five game domains without task-specific fine-tuning.

  5. 05

    Enabling Agentic PCG with Harness-Controlled Local Models

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Ahmed Khalifa, Julian Togelius

    An execution harness enables local language models to recover incomplete tool calls, use feedback, and retain successful level edits without changing their weights. Controlled experiments evaluate the harness and compare high-level PCG tools with tile-by-tile editing on structural design tasks, measuring target matching, structural success, and playability.

  6. 06

    Making generation cheaper while keeping control

    Adapting small models

    Exploring whether language models with fewer than four billion parameters can reliably generate and revise levels, bringing natural-language PCG to edge devices with limited memory and compute.

    Reusable level pieces

    Discovering reusable level pieces with diverse functional properties, such as jump patterns, enemy encounters, and item collection. We aim to turn these pieces into generation tools and explore how construction strategies from larger models can be captured in reusable components.

    Player agents for richer feedback

    Developing compact player agents that evaluate levels through gameplay, providing richer feedback on requested behaviors and helping generators produce more varied, playable content.

PCGRLLM

In-Chang Baek, Sung-Hyun Kim, Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Jin-Ha Noh, Julian Togelius, Kyung-Joong Kim

Uses language models to turn story-like instructions into reward functions for reinforcement-learning level generators. Feedback from generated content helps refine the rewards and bring the resulting levels closer to the intended design.

Paper

Alpha-Wolves and Alpha-Mammals

Sudipta Banerjee, Anubhav Jain, Zehua Jiang, Nasir Memon, Julian Togelius, Arun Ross

Explores dictionary attacks on iris recognition systems. Combining iris templates into alpha-wolves and alpha-mammals reveals how a small set of mixed templates can falsely match multiple identities, exposing a vulnerability in biometric matching.

Paper

Controllable Path of Destruction

Matthew Siper, Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Ahmed Khalifa, Julian Togelius

Learns to generate by repairing: destroying example artifacts produces training trajectories whose reverse actions teach a generator to rebuild them. Conditional inputs let designers steer the results, with experiments on 2D dungeons and 3D LEGO cars.

Paper

MasterPrint2: Diversity and Novelty MasterPrints

M Charity, Nasir Memon, Zehua Jiang, Abhi Sen, Julian Togelius

Extends DeepMasterPrints with quality-diversity search to generate sets of synthetic fingerprints. Diversity and novelty objectives seek prints that match different users, increasing the combined coverage of the set and revealing vulnerabilities in fingerprint recognition.
