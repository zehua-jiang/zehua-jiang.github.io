About

Hi, I'm Zehua.

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at New York University, advised by Prof. Julian Togelius at the NYU Game Innovation Lab. Before that I earned an M.S. in Electrical Engineering at New York University and a B.S. in Optoelectronic Information Science and Engineering at Sun Yat-sen University, including an exchange semester at UC Berkeley.

My research focuses on procedural content generation: how agents learn or reuse strategies for building playable levels from design goals. I study reinforcement learning, language-model tool use, and reusable constructions, with an emphasis on control, generalization, reliable execution, and computational cost. The aim is to support game creation and provide varied environments for training and evaluating agents. Earlier I worked on synthetic fingerprints and irises.

Outside research I am a traveler, photographer and video editor.

Research question

How do agents learn and reuse strategies for building game levels?

A generator needs to know which edits produce a traversable path, a solvable puzzle, or a requested gameplay experience. My dissertation studies this construction knowledge when game rules and an executable environment are available but authored examples are limited. I examine where the knowledge comes from and how observations, tools, and feedback let an agent use it.

Learned from feedback

Editing policies learn by building levels and receiving task rewards, without a corpus of authored examples. Their observations shape which strategies transfer to new targets, map sizes, and shapes.

Carried from pretraining

Pretrained language models select editing tools and generation algorithms, observe the results, and revise the level. This supports numeric targets and broader language requests without task-specific fine-tuning.

Encoded in tools

Generation algorithms and tested level pieces supply useful constructions directly. My ongoing work asks whether search can discover reusable pieces and reduce the manual work needed to support a model.

What an agent can build depends on both its construction knowledge and the process that lets it use that knowledge. Across my studies, observations, available tools, and environmental feedback shape control, generalization, and generation cost.

Research arc

From learned policies to tool-using agents

Five collaborative projects connect learned editing policies, tool-using language models, and reliable local execution. The ongoing work asks whether smaller adapted models and automatically discovered level pieces can make generation more practical while preserving control.

  1. 01

    Learning Controllable 3D Level Generators

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Michael Cerny Green, Julian Togelius

    Extends PCGRL to Minecraft tasks with jumping and gravity. Editing policies learn structures such as staircases from task feedback and adapt them to different fixed-door configurations, without authored level examples. Control is stronger for shorter path targets and remains limited for jump counts and large vertical separations.

  2. 02

    Scaling, Control and Generalization in Reinforcement Learning Level Generators

    Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Julian Togelius

    A JAX implementation moves simulation and training onto the GPU. We study observation size, randomized map shapes, and fixed positions for key tiles. Local observations improve generalization on the tested unseen maps, while global observations can perform better on maps matching the training setup.

  3. 03

    Video Game Level Design as a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Problem

    Sam Earle, Zehua Jiang, Eugene Vinitsky, Julian Togelius

    Several agents with a shared policy and reward edit the same level. Their joint steps let multiple edits share an expensive reward computation. Some advantages shrink when total actions and reward computations are held constant, while benefits persist on tested maps with unfamiliar randomized shapes.

  4. 04

    Agentic PCG: Procedural Content Generation via Tool-using LLMs

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Ahmed Khalifa, Julian Togelius

    A language model builds and revises levels through tools, using symbolic observations and feedback after each action. Individual tile edits and higher-level generation algorithms share one interface, with evaluators deciding which revisions to keep. The framework supports computable targets and broader language requests across five game domains without task-specific fine-tuning.

  5. 05

    Enabling Agentic PCG with Harness-Controlled Local Models

    Zehua Jiang, Sam Earle, Ahmed Khalifa, Julian Togelius

    An execution harness helps local models recover from incomplete tool calls and preserve useful edits without changing their weights. Tested platformer builders also support requests about jumps, coins, and enemy defeats. The system remains limited by what its tools and evaluators can represent, and local generation is still too slow for use during gameplay.

  6. 06

    Making generation cheaper while keeping control

    Adapting small models

    Investigating models with fewer than four billion parameters. Initial supervised fine-tuning showed signs of overfitting; the plan now examines RL with verifiable rewards and on-policy distillation, while holding the tools and execution harness fixed.

    Reusable level pieces

    Investigating whether quality-diversity search, starting with MAP-Elites, can discover pieces that support jumps, enemy defeats, and item collection. A simulated player would test their behavior and how they fit together before an agent reuses them.

    Player agents for richer feedback

    An exploratory direction: adapting a small model to choose valid gameplay actions quickly. More capable evaluation players could demonstrate a wider range of requested behaviors and give the level-building agent more useful feedback.
